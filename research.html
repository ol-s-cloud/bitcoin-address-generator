COBRA RESEARCH

Cryptographic control is a technical and human question.

COBRA examines how keys are created, protected and used; how public ledgers expose information; how cryptographic systems withstand technological change; and how people preserve claims on value across time.

ECONOMIC HISTORY

How value survives across systems

COBRA treats cryptocurrency as one chapter in a longer history of scarcity, institutional trust, credit and verifiable control.

Commodity and metallic systems

Physical scarcity and durable material properties constrained supply and supported exchange.

Gold-linked monetary regimes

Currency convertibility linked monetary claims to a reserve asset under changing institutional arrangements.

Fiat, credit and debt

Modern money depends on sovereign institutions, commercial banks, payment networks, taxation, law and confidence.

Cryptographic property

Public-key cryptography permits digitally verifiable control without requiring physical possession of the asset represented.
