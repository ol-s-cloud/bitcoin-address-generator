VALUE SYSTEMS · ECONOMIC HISTORY

From commodity money to cryptographic property.

COBRA's research is not limited to cryptocurrency as “money.” It asks a broader question: how do societies preserve claims on value across institutions, technologies and generations?

Commodity & metallic systems

Historically, commodities and precious metals provided monetary anchors because scarcity and physical properties constrained supply.

Gold-linked monetary regimes

Various monetary systems linked currency to gold at different points in history. These arrangements changed substantially during the twentieth century.

Modern fiat systems

Major currencies today are not generally redeemable for a fixed quantity of gold. Their operation depends on sovereign monetary institutions, taxation, banking systems, legal frameworks and economic confidence.

Credit & debt

Modern economies rely heavily on bank credit, government and private debt, payment networks and interconnected balance sheets. COBRA treats claims such as “the economy is debt” as questions to analyse precisely rather than slogans.

Cryptographic value

Public-key cryptography introduced a different possibility: digitally verifiable control that can operate independently of physical possession and, in some systems, without a central ledger operator.
